How the score works

Nine checks, weighted to 100. Nothing secret.

Weights v0.2 (July 2026) are grounded in the published evidence — the GEO paper (KDD 2024), Ahrefs' schema intervention test (May 2026), the Vercel/MERJ crawler study (Dec 2024), Google's generative-AI guidance (May 2026), the SE Ranking llms.txt null result (Nov 2025) — plus our own quoted-vs-control citation tests. We publish every change, including the signals that turned out not to matter.

The 9 checks & their weight

Each check earns a fraction of its weight based on how well the page performs. Weights are normalized to a 0–100 score.

AI crawler access
Can AI bots fetch the page at all?
25

Gate condition: a page that GPTBot, ClaudeBot or PerplexityBot cannot fetch can never be cited, regardless of quality. Highest weight in the rubric.

Answer-first (BLUF)
Is the answer in the first 150 words?
12

AI engines lift self-contained passages. Pages that state the answer up top get quoted; pages that bury it under a long intro get skipped or paraphrased without credit.

JSON-LD schema
Structured data an engine can parse.
8

JSON-LD is the cleanest machine-readable statement of what a page is, who wrote it and when. High-value types (Article, FAQPage, Product) map directly onto answer surfaces.

Heading hierarchy
Clean, ordered H1–H3 outline.
10

Headings are how engines chunk a page into liftable passages. A broken outline (multiple H1s, skipped levels) breaks that chunking.

Author / E-E-A-T
Named author, dates, credentials.
9

Engines deciding whom to quote lean on named authorship and freshness. Anonymous, undated pages are quoted last.

Extractable Q&A
Question headings with tight answers.
10

Question-shaped sections map one-to-one onto the questions people ask AI engines. A question heading followed by a tight answer is the most liftable unit on the web.

Named-statistic density
Concrete, quotable facts and figures.
12

When an engine cites a page it usually quotes a concrete claim — a number, a date, a named finding. Vague prose gives it nothing to quote.

Content-to-markup ratio
Real text vs. wrapper markup.
12

Engines tokenize your raw HTML. Div soup and script-heavy shells bury the content; some JS-only pages ship no content at all.

llms.txt
Optional AI-guidance manifest.
2

Honestly weighted: we have not observed llms.txt moving citation behavior yet, so it carries the lowest weight in the rubric. Cheap to add, small upside, no penalty drama.

What we don't do

No keyword stuffing advice, no backlink games, no black-box AI score. We only report signals you can verify in your own page source — and we show you exactly where each one came from.

What the score can't measure

Domain authority. In our live tests, engines cited major banks, hospitals and .gov sites even when their on-page readiness scored an F. LLMScout measures the on-page half you control; it predicts citations best among comparable-authority sites competing on the same topic — which is exactly where most sites compete.

How a scan runs

STEP 1
Fetch as a bot

We request the page the way an AI crawler would — no JavaScript execution — and check whether we even get in.

STEP 2
Parse the raw HTML

Headings, schema, dates, authorship and text are pulled straight from source, not a rendered screenshot.

STEP 3
Score each check

Every check returns a 0–1 quality score, multiplied by its weight. Nothing is subjective or model-guessed.

STEP 4
Rank the fixes

We sort gaps by impact × effort so the top of your list is always the fastest route to a higher grade.
